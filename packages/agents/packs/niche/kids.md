# Kids niche pack

## Customer
- Segments: new/expecting parents 25–38 (research-heavy, safety-first, registries); toddler/preschool parents (development, screen-free, Montessori); parents of 6–12 (fun, kid requests, STEM); gift givers (grandparents 55+, aunts/uncles, high AOV, Facebook); teachers/homeschoolers.
- Care about: safety, age fit, durability, educational value, easy cleaning, gift presentation.
- Hang out: Instagram mom creators, TikTok (#momtok), Pinterest, Facebook parent groups, Reddit (r/Parenting, r/toddlers), YouTube toy reviews.
- Motivation: solving a pain point (sleep, mealtime mess, travel), development, kid demand, gifting, milestones.

## Buying triggers and objections
- Triggers: "busy for an hour, no screens", picky eater/bedtime fixes, birthday or holiday deadline, viral "toy every kid wants", age milestone.
- "Is it safe?" → certification (CPSIA/ASTM F963 tested, lab name), age grade, materials, choking warnings.
- "Will they actually play with it?" → real kid play footage, age range.
- "Breaks fast" → durability demo, warranty.
- "Here before the birthday/Christmas?" → ETA and cutoff dates.

## Compliance and claims
- Children's product = designed primarily for kids 12 and under → CPSIA: testing at a CPSC-accepted third-party lab, a Children's Product Certificate (CPC) issued by the US importer or manufacturer, permanent tracking labels on product and packaging. Assume your store carries this obligation (verify).
- Lead ≤100 ppm substrate, ≤90 ppm paint/coatings; 8 phthalates capped at 0.1% in toys and child care articles.
- Toys: ASTM F963 mandatory. No small parts for under 3 (16 CFR 1501). Choking warnings (16 CFR 1500.19) for ages 3–6 toys with small parts/balls/marbles/balloons, on packaging and the listing.
- Magnets: 16 CFR 1262. Button batteries: Reese's Law (16 CFR 1263): secured compartments, warnings, child-resistant packaging.
- Nursery (cribs, bassinets, high chairs, carriers, walkers, etc.): product-specific CPSC standards plus registration cards. Safe Sleep for Babies Act bans inclined sleepers and padded crib bumpers. Avoid weighted infant sleep products and positioners.
- Sleepwear (sizes 9 mo–14): flame-resistant or tight-fitting with label (16 CFR 1615/1616). No hood/neck drawstrings on outerwear 2T–12.
- Never dropship car seats (FMVSS 213), baby food/formula, amber teething necklaces, water beads, infant neck floats.
- Claims: no medical/developmental promises ("cures colic", "prevents SIDS", "boosts IQ", "helps autism/ADHD"). "Montessori-inspired" is fine. "Non-toxic", "BPA-free", "organic" (GOTS) only with reports/certificates.
- CPSC eFiling of certificate data at import phased in from July 2026; verify.
- Platforms: Amazon/TikTok Shop require test reports for toys and baby. Ads target parents, never children; COPPA if collecting data from under-13s.
- EU/UK: CE/UKCA + EN 71, GPSR responsible person; new EU Toy Safety Regulation (verify timeline).

## Seasonality
- Jan–Feb: slow; toy storage; Chinese New Year supply gap.
- Mar–Apr: Easter baskets (small toys, books).
- May–Jun: outdoor/water play, end of school, summer travel kits.
- Jul–Aug: back-to-school (backpacks, lunch, clothing); Prime Day toy discounting.
- Sep–Oct: Halloween costumes; holiday toy lists publish in Oct; holiday creative live late Oct.
- Nov–Dec: often 40–60% of annual toy revenue (BFCM, Christmas, Hanukkah). Cutoffs ~Dec 15 domestic, late Nov China-direct.
- Prep: lab testing takes 2–4+ weeks; certify Q4 SKUs by Aug, land US stock by Oct.

## Creative angles
- Formats: real kid play UGC (written parental consent), parent "hack" problem/solution, mess before/after, educator explainer (no medical claims), age-based gift guides.
- Proof: safety-test badge, parent reviews, real awards only.
- Angles:
  1. "This kept my 3-year-old busy for 45 minutes, no screens".
  2. "Mealtime before vs after": suction plate demo.
  3. "Gift for the kid who has everything (ages 4–7)".
  4. "Why I switched to these": materials story backed by test data.
  5. "Road-trip survival kit".

## Price norms and margins
- Bands: stocking stuffers $10–25; core toys/learning $25–60; nursery/baby gear $40–150; kids' apparel $15–40.
- AOV: $45–80; gifting lifts Q4 AOV ~15–25%.
- Landed COGS: 25–35%, plus third-party testing (roughly $300–2,000+ per SKU/material; get quotes). Duties apply to every import since US de minimis ended (Aug 2025).
- Compare-at: 20–40%, genuine former price only.
- Bundles: age-based kits, sibling bundles, gift wrap upsell ($3–8), craft refill subscriptions.

## Products and suppliers
- Evergreen: wooden/Montessori toys, arts and crafts, sensory toys, silicone feeding, bath toys, backpacks/lunch.
- Trend: viral toys and collectibles (often licensed IP), 1–3 month windows.
- Quality pitfalls: chipping paint, detachable small parts, sharp edges, loose magnets, unsecured battery doors, non-food-grade silicone, moldy bath toys.
- Return drivers: wrong age fit, broken on arrival, smaller than expected (show scale), lost interest.
- Shipping: retail-ready gift boxes that survive transit.
- Vetting: recent (≤12 months) test reports from a CPSC-accepted lab matching your exact SKU and materials; order samples; prefer suppliers already exporting to US/EU retail.

## Red flags
- No CPC or test report; "CE" alone is not US compliance.
- Unlicensed characters (Disney, Pokemon, Bluey).
- Any banned or never-dropship item listed above.
- Medical or developmental claims; kids in ads without consent.
