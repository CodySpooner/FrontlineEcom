# Beauty niche pack

## Customer
- Ingredient-literate skincare buyers (20-40): read INCI lists, compare % actives, distrust hype.
- Anti-aging 35-60: highest AOV/LTV; buy routines, devices (LED, microcurrent), gift sets.
- Acne-prone and sensitive skin: fear breakouts and irritation; buy on gentleness and fragrance-free.
- Haircare, nails (press-ons, gel kits), makeup, men's grooming: separate vocabularies; one segment per ad.
- Teens/tweens: never target minors in ads or push strong actives at them.
- Hangouts: TikTok (SkinTok, TikTok Shop lives), Instagram Reels, YouTube, Pinterest, Reddit (r/SkincareAddiction, r/AsianBeauty).
- Motivation: self-care, a specific concern, a viral product, gifting.

## Buying triggers and objections
- Triggers: viral texture video, visible same-day effect, trusted creator, "dupe" of a cult product, replenishment reminder.
- "Will it break me out?" -> full INCI list, fragrance-free flag, patch-test steps, % actives.
- "Fake or expired?" -> batch code, expiry/PAO, sealed packaging, US warehouse.
- "Does it work?" -> week 1 vs week 4 UGC in the same light, photo reviews, money-back window.
- "Wrong shade" -> shade finder, swatches on several skin tones.
- "Cheaper at the drugstore" -> concentration, routine value, refills, gift-with-purchase.

## Compliance and claims
- FDA: cosmetics cleanse, beautify or alter appearance; claims to treat, prevent or change body structure/function make it a drug. OK: "reduces the look of fine lines", "visibly brighter", "hydrates". Not OK: "repairs collagen", "heals", "treats acne/eczema/rosacea", "regrows hair", "removes scars".
- OTC drugs in the US (Drug Facts label, registered drug facility): sunscreen/SPF, acne treatments, anti-dandruff, antiperspirant, hair regrowth.
- MoCRA (2022): facility registration, product listing, safety substantiation, serious adverse event reports to FDA within 15 business days, US contact info on label; some small-business exemptions. The name on the label is the responsible person. Verify current deadlines.
- Label: identity, net quantity, INCI ingredients in descending order, business name/address, warnings. Color additives must be approved for the use (eye area stricter).
- Devices (LED masks, microcurrent, RF, IPL/laser hair removal, microneedling) are FDA medical devices for most claims. Say "FDA-cleared" only with a 510(k) for that exact model and indication. Heated hair tools: UL/ETL listing.
- "Clinically proven", "dermatologist tested", "hypoallergenic", "non-comedogenic" need substantiation on file.
- FTC: disclose paid/gifted creators, no fake or bought reviews (2024 Consumer Review rule), typical results only, no filters on results.
- Ads: Meta/TikTok restrict before/after and personal-attribute copy ("Do you have acne?"); write "for acne-prone skin".
- EU/UK: Responsible Person, CPNP/SCPN notification, safety report, longer banned-ingredient list; teeth whitening above 0.1% H2O2 is professional-only.
- California and other states ban some ingredients (certain PFAS, formaldehyde releasers); check Prop 65 per SKU.

## Seasonality
- Jan: skin reset, barrier care. Feb: Valentine's lip, fragrance, gift sets.
- Mar-Apr: spring refresh; big-retailer beauty sales pressure prices.
- May: Mother's Day (2nd Sunday), top non-Q4 gifting; creatives ready mid-April.
- Apr-Jun: prom and weddings (lashes, nails, glam). Jun-Aug: SPF, body care, sweat-proof makeup, hair removal; Prime Day (July).
- Aug: back-to-school (teen acne, makeup). Sep-Oct: retinol season, Halloween makeup.
- Q4: advent calendars and gift sets (launch Sep-Oct, order by Jul-Aug), BFCM, party glam, stocking stuffers under $30.

## Creative angles
- Texture/application close-ups; show the effect in the first 2 seconds.
- Week 1 vs week 4 UGC: same light and angle, unfiltered.
- Esthetician ingredient breakdowns; factual dupe side-by-sides; routine stacking to sell kits.
- Examples: "I cut 6 products down to this 3-step routine"; "Pores 10 seconds after (no filter)"; "4 weeks of red light on my texture"; "A gift set she'll actually use"; "Press-ons that lasted 2 weeks".

## Price norms and margins
- Skincare $18-$45; serums $25-$60; routine kits $50-$120; tools $15-$40; press-ons $10-$25; hair tools $40-$150; LED/microcurrent $80-$400.
- AOV $45-$75; free-shipping threshold $35-$50.
- Landed COGS: private-label skincare 10-25% of price; devices/tools 20-35%.
- Bundles lift AOV 20-40%; subscribe-and-save 10-15% off; replenishment every 30-60 days.
- Compare-at only for a real recent price.

## Products and suppliers
- Evergreen: barrier care, lip care, haircare, nails, gua sha/ice rollers, heated lash curlers. Trend: K-beauty actives (snail mucin, PDRN, rice), tallow balms, LED masks, peel-off lip stains; test before deep stock.
- Pitfalls: no INCI/COA, unstable formulas, contamination (mercury, hydroquinone in imports), counterfeits, missing batch/expiry.
- Shipping: heat melts balms and splits emulsions; shrink bands, pump locks, padded glass; US 3PL (2-5 days).
- Return drivers: reactions, shade mismatch, "no results". Opened cosmetics aren't resellable; refund without return on low-cost items.
- Vet: GMP lab, stability and preservative tests, MoCRA registration, batch traceability, samples before scaling.

## Red flags
- Skin lighteners (hydroquinone, mercury), Rx actives (tretinoin), prostaglandin lash serums, colored contacts without prescription.
- Hyaluron pens, DIY mole/skin tag removers, deep microneedling pens.
- "FDA approved", "medical grade", "cures", "chemical-free" claims.
- Filtered results, fake reviews, undisclosed creator posts, 10-20 day China-direct skincare.
