# Tech niche pack

## Customer
- Gadget enthusiasts (18-40, male-skewed): spec-driven, cross-check Amazon and YouTube.
- Desk-setup, WFH and gamers: aesthetics plus productivity (monitor lights, keyboards, docks, cable management).
- Mainstream problem-solvers: mounts, chargers, trackers, smart plugs; want "it just works".
- Gift buyers (non-technical): Q4-heavy; need compatibility certainty, fast shipping, easy returns.
- Hangouts: YouTube reviews, TikTok (TechTok, "Amazon finds"), Reddit (r/gadgets, r/battlestations), X, deal sites.
- Motivation: fix an annoyance, accessorize a new device, novelty, gifting.

## Buying triggers and objections
- Triggers: satisfying demo of a clever function, new phone launch, price drop, gift-ready packaging.
- "Will it work with my device?" -> compatibility matrix (models, OS, ports), wattage and protocol (USB PD, Qi2), Find My vs Android.
- "Cheap junk?" -> 12+ month warranty, certifications shown, real measured specs, stress test.
- "Is it safe?" (chargers, batteries) -> UL/ETL listing, overheat protection, FCC ID.
- "Cheaper on Amazon" -> assume they check; win on bundle, warranty or spec, or skip commodity SKUs.

## Compliance and claims
- FCC: Bluetooth/WiFi/RF devices need an FCC ID; other digital devices need a Supplier's Declaration of Conformity. Match the FCC ID to the exact model. Selling unauthorized RF devices is illegal; jammers are banned outright.
- Safety listing (UL/ETL): rarely federally required, but expected by marketplaces, insurers and buyers. Essential for chargers (UL 62368-1), batteries/power banks (UL 2054/62133), e-bikes/scooters (UL 2849/2272; NYC requires it). Fake marks are common; verify in the certifier's directory.
- External power supplies: DOE Level VI efficiency mark.
- Button/coin batteries: Reese's Law (16 CFR 1263) requires secured compartments, warnings, child-resistant packaging.
- Lithium shipping: every cell/battery needs a UN38.3 test summary and SDS. Standalone batteries (power banks, UN3480) are forbidden as cargo on passenger aircraft; batteries in or with equipment (UN3481) ship with limits; over 100Wh is fully regulated dangerous goods. USPS: standalone lithium batteries ground-only domestically, restricted internationally. Many China lines refuse or delay battery goods. Verify carrier rules.
- Claims must match test data: mAh/Wh, ANSI lumens, native vs "supported" resolution, IP rating, "military grade" (MIL-STD-810 report), ANC vs ENC.
- "MFi", "Qi2", "Find My" only with certification. Use brand names for factual compatibility ("fits iPhone 16"), never their logos.
- Lasers: FDA product rules; pointers over 5mW can't be sold as pointers. Drones: FAA registration over 250g, Remote ID.
- EU/UK: CE/UKCA, Radio Equipment Directive, RoHS, WEEE, EU Battery Regulation, USB-C common charger; UK PSTI for connected devices (no default passwords, stated support period).
- Prop 65 warnings common on cables and plastics.

## Seasonality
- Jan: accessories for gifted devices, CES trend cycle, desk reset.
- Feb-Mar: Samsung flagship launch; tax refunds lift bigger tickets.
- May-Jun: graduation, Father's Day (3rd Sunday June).
- Jul: Prime Day. Jul-Aug: back-to-school (laptop gear, dorm tech); summer speakers, fans, power stations.
- Sep-Oct: iPhone launch drives cases, protectors, chargers; don't pre-produce on leaked dimensions.
- Oct: fall Prime event. Nov: BFCM, biggest tech week, deal-driven. Dec: gifts under $50; after mid-Dec, delivery promise beats discount.
- Battery goods by sea/ground need 6-10 weeks; land Q4 stock by early October.

## Creative angles
- 3-second "wait, it does that?" demo; function before brand.
- Side-by-side vs a known product with honest test conditions.
- Desk transformations, unboxings, stress tests within the rated IP level.
- Examples: "This replaced 4 chargers on my nightstand"; "My whole desk setup under $100"; "Projector test with the lights on"; "Lost my keys 3 times, never again"; "Tested 5 power banks, one hit its rated capacity".

## Price norms and margins
- Accessories (cables, mounts, cases) $10-$35; impulse gadgets $20-$60; audio $30-$150; smart home $25-$100; projectors/power stations $80-$500.
- AOV $45-$80. Instant Amazon price-checking caps margin on commodity SKUs.
- Landed COGS 25-45%; gross margin 35-60% on differentiated gadgets.
- Upsells: cables/adapters, cases, extended warranty (5-15% attach), 2-pack discount.
- Compare-at only for real prior prices.

## Products and suppliers
- Evergreen: chargers, cables, mounts, desk lighting, smart plugs, trackers, keyboards, cases.
- Trend: magnetic power banks, mini thermal printers, AI voice recorders, smart rings, translation earbuds, retro handhelds; 2-6 month lifecycles, test small.
- Pitfalls: inflated specs, fake certifications, DOA units, abandoned companion apps, fake-capacity storage.
- Return drivers: incompatibility, not as advertised, defects (budget 3-8%), remorse. Opened electronics rarely resell as new.
- Shipping: battery restrictions; foam for screens; US stock before Q4.
- Vet: FCC ID, safety listing, UN38.3 + SDS, sample-test real specs (capacity tester, lux meter), app ownership and support, supplier warranty terms.

## Red flags
- Signal/GPS jammers, hidden cameras, high-power lasers.
- ROM-loaded handhelds, IPTV/"jailbroken" streaming boxes.
- Knockoff AirPods or branded goods, fake MFi or UL marks.
- Uncertified power banks, hoverboards/e-bikes without UL, fake-capacity SD cards/SSDs.
- Vapes, kids' smartwatches with weak privacy (COPPA).
