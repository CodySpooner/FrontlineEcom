# Outdoor niche pack

## Customer
- Segments: car campers and families 25–45; hikers/backpackers (weight-obsessed, research-heavy); anglers and hunters (mostly men 30–60, Facebook/YouTube); emergency-preparedness buyers.
- Care about: reliability, weight and packed size, durability, real specs (lumens, Wh, IPX, temp rating), warranty, value vs REI/Amazon brands.
- Hang out: YouTube gear reviews, Reddit (r/CampingGear, r/Ultralight), TikTok camp hacks, Facebook fishing/hunting groups.
- Motivation: upcoming trip, replacing failed gear, season start, gifts for dads, outages and storms.

## Buying triggers and objections
- Triggers: trip booked, season opener, gear failure, viral camp hack, storm/outage news, Father's Day, holidays.
- "Will it hold up?" → field footage, torture tests (drop, rain, freeze), 1–2+ year warranty.
- "Are the specs real?" → measured runtime, lumen and waterproof tests on camera; never inflate lumens or mAh.
- "Why not a known brand?" → side-by-side spec/weight table, warranty, US support.
- "Too heavy/bulky" → weight on a scale (oz and g), packed size next to a water bottle.

## Compliance and claims
- Knives: the federal Switchblade Act bars interstate shipment of automatic knives (assisted-opening exempt). State/city rules on autos, balisongs, daggers, blade length vary; geo-block where needed. UK requires age verification at delivery for bladed items.
- Ads: Meta, TikTok and Google restrict weapons: tactical knives, batons, stun guns, sprays, crossbows, firearm accessories usually rejected; camp knives case by case (verify). TikTok Shop prohibits most.
- Sprays: pepper and bear spray are hazmat aerosols, ground only. Bear spray is EPA-registered (FIFRA). Several states restrict self-defense sprays; verify.
- Fuel: gas canisters, fire starters, strike-anywhere matches, flares are hazmat: ground only under a carrier hazmat agreement; rarely dropship-viable.
- Lithium (headlamps, power banks, power stations, heated gear): UN38.3 test summary required. Standalone batteries (UN3480) ship under stricter rules than in-equipment (UN3481); air ~100 Wh cap (verify); big power stations go ground. Electronics need FCC Part 15; power stations should carry UL 2743.
- Flammability: some states have required CPAI-84 flame-resistance labels on tents (verify). Warn against indoor stove/heater use (CO).
- Safety gear: bike helmets must meet CPSC 16 CFR 1203 with certification label. "Life jacket/PFD" only if USCG-approved. Climbing gear needs UIAA/EN certification; never imply life-safety for uncertified gear.
- Claims: water-filter removal claims need NSF/ANSI or EPA-protocol testing; insect repellents need EPA registration (no ultrasonic/bracelet claims); UPF needs AATCC 183 testing; "waterproof" needs a tested IPX rating or hydrostatic head (mm).
- Prop 65 (PVC, brass, lead); lead fishing tackle restricted in some states (verify).
- EU/UK: PPE Regulation 2016/425 (helmets, harnesses, PFDs), CE/UKCA, GPSR.

## Seasonality
- Jan–Feb: trip planning, winter sports, ice fishing; Chinese New Year supply gap.
- Mar–Apr: fishing openers, spring break.
- May–Jun: Memorial Day starts camping season; Father's Day (3rd Sunday of June) is the biggest gift window outside Q4.
- Jul: July 4, Prime Day; peak usage.
- Aug–Sep: Labor Day last camping push; hunting prep (Aug–Oct); hurricane season (Jun–Nov) spikes emergency gear.
- Oct–Dec: hunting, winter gear (heated apparel, warmers), BFCM, power stations.
- Prep: summer stock landed by April; shoot in-field creative the season before; keep factual outage/storm creatives ready.

## Creative angles
- Formats: POV field demos, torture tests, specs vs category leader (no trademark misuse), timed setup.
- Proof: measured tests on camera, customer trip photos, warranty.
- Angles:
  1. "I left it in the creek overnight": waterproof proof.
  2. "Setup in 47 seconds, timer on screen": tents/awnings.
  3. "Power went out; this ran my fridge for 14 hours": real measured test.
  4. "Half the weight of my old one": ultralight swap on a scale.
  5. "For the dad who says he needs nothing".

## Price norms and margins
- Bands: gadgets/accessories $15–45; lighting $20–60; apparel $40–150; packs/tents/sleep systems $60–300; power stations $200–1,200.
- AOV: $70–130.
- Landed COGS: 30–45% (heavy, bulky freight); price at 2.5–3x landed. US de minimis ended Aug 2025; include duty and current tariffs (verify).
- Compare-at: 15–30%, genuine only; buyers price-check REI/Amazon.
- Bundles: camp kitchen and emergency kits, add-ons (stuff sacks, batteries, carabiners), extended warranty.

## Products and suppliers
- Evergreen: lanterns, headlamps, camp kitchen, hammocks, chairs, bottles, non-weapon multitools, emergency kits.
- Trend: viral gadgets (portable showers, mini stoves, solar panels).
- Quality pitfalls: inflated lumens/mAh/waterproof ratings, leaking seams, zipper failure, weak poles, battery fade, sleeping-bag temps quoting limit instead of comfort rating (ISO 23537).
- Return drivers: field failure, specs below claims, bigger/heavier than expected.
- Shipping: dimensional weight on bulky items; hazmat (batteries, fuel, aerosols) limits carriers.
- Vetting: field-test samples; request UN38.3, SDS, IP test reports, helmet certification; confirm warranty/spare-parts support.

## Red flags
- Switchblades, balisongs, brass knuckles, batons, stun guns; "tactical" weapon framing.
- Uncertified helmets, harnesses, PFDs or life-safety claims.
- Unregistered repellent or purification claims; inflated specs.
- Fear-driven prepper ads or price spikes during disasters (price-gouging laws).
